How this works and what to keep in mind
  • The square foot estimate uses the lowest, middle and highest base price per sq ft from 5 full inspections, which span 3,000 to 8,000 sf. The 35,000 sf Two Harbors job is left out of the rates because 5 of its units were not built yet.
  • The unit estimate uses the base price per unit from the 3 apartment buildings. When both estimates are available, the suggested price is their average, rounded to the nearest $25.
  • Time is worked out in inspector-hours, which is on-site duration times the number of inspectors for each past job. The calculator estimates total inspector-hours the same two ways as price (by square feet and by units), averages them, then divides by the recommended crew to get duration.
  • Crew size is 2 by default, since all 5 full inspections used 2 or 3 inspectors. It goes to 3 at 8 or more apartment units (the only 8-unit job used 3) or when the estimate reaches 13 inspector-hours (the 3-inspector job took 13.5). That rule rests on one job, so treat it as a suggestion.
  • Duration and inspector-hours include time spent on add-ons, since the export does not separate it.
  • Scope was read from the order notes. Pine City was not labeled full, so it is included on the assumption that a whole-building inspection was done.
  • A 5-point inspection takes 15 minutes per 1,000 sq ft off the full inspection's total inspector-hours. The crew is then resized from the remaining inspector-hours: up to 4 is 1 inspector, up to 8 is 2, more than 8 is 3, but never more than a full inspection of the same building would use.
  • A 5-point inspection is priced as the full inspection quote minus $0.10 per sq ft. The range shown is the full inspection range minus the same amount.
  • This is a starting number for the team to sanity check. Outside 3,000 to 8,000 sf or 4 to 8 units, there are no full inspections to compare against, and the page says so.
